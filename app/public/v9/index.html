01The field

First establish the scale of the medical problem.

Germline editing enters a field that is already medically active. To see where it matters, the first task is to define the burden into which it would enter. On the paper’s default assumptions, an annual global birth cohort of about — contains a serious genetic-disease birth-cohort burden of about —.

That total has two very different parts: about — serious monogenic affected births and — serious multifactorial congenital cases. The multifactorial component is anchored to congenital disease measured around birth; it is not a lifetime-incidence count for coronary disease, diabetes, psychiatric disease or Alzheimer disease.

The denominator is therefore a common birth-cohort reference scale, not a census of every genetically influenced disease across a lifetime. Making that unit explicit is what allows later percentages to remain interpretable when the analysis moves from monogenic disease to future polygenic possibilities.

02What already changes outcomes

Before adding a new intervention, calculate what the existing system can already do.

Serious monogenic disease gives the cleanest present comparison because causal variants, inheritance and reproductive options are often well defined. Carrier screening can identify risk before pregnancy; PGT-M can select among embryos; prenatal diagnosis can identify an affected pregnancy; newborn screening and treatment act later, after an affected birth has occurred.

When the reproductive pathways are modeled sequentially, current coverage is associated with avoidance of about — of serious monogenic affected births. Expanding access raises the modeled fraction to —; idealized full coverage reaches —. The last figure is a technical horizon rather than an expectation of universal uptake.

The distance between current and idealized coverage is itself a medical result. It means that a large share of present opportunity still lies in finding risk earlier, interpreting variants correctly, making reproductive and diagnostic services available, and delivering treatment in time — infrastructure that any future germline-editing programme would also need.

03Where editing changes the outcome

The strongest present case begins where selection reaches a biological limit.

PGT-M can choose among the embryos that exist; it cannot create an unaffected embryo when none is available. Excluding congenital deafness as a contested prevention category, the model identifies reproductive configurations corresponding to about — otherwise-affected births per year in which no unaffected embryo can be selected.

The same number carries two kinds of significance. At population scale it is small — roughly 0.8% of serious monogenic disease. For a family in that group, however, the marginal value can be much larger: a successful correction could in principle create a route to an unaffected child genetically related to both intended parents where selection cannot. Donor gametes, adoption and choosing not to reproduce remain genuine alternatives, but they answer a different reproductive aim.

Reproductive need is only the first gate. A molecular correction route must exist; it must work reliably in a human embryo; and the intervention must become safe enough to justify heritable clinical use. The evidence thins as the pathway approaches those later gates, so the analysis leaves them open rather than filling them with a generic editing-efficiency number.

04Calculate the comparator

Selection has a burden before it has a limit.

Saying that “PGT is available” hides part of the comparison. If u is the fraction of embryos acceptable with respect to the target genotype, the expected number that fail that target for every acceptable embryo is (1-u)/u. When half are acceptable the ratio is 1; at 20% it is 4; at 5% it is 19. Selection can remain technically possible while becoming increasingly demanding in embryo numbers, cycles and time.

The structural ratio is only the beginning of the clinical pathway. Contemporary PGT-M data give it an empirical scale: one 572-cycle cohort averaged 4.10 biopsied embryos per stimulated cycle and 40.2% live births per stimulated cycle, while the systematic review across 51 studies found a 29.7% birth rate per stimulated cycle overall.

Embryo non-selection also cannot be read as embryo destruction. In ESHRE data from 2019–2021, 1,198 of 2,576 embryos classified as non-genetically-transferable for the PGT-M/PGT-SR target remained cryopreserved. Final disposition is a separate empirical question.

Five independent criteria each met by half of embryos would leave a joint acceptable fraction of 1/32 and 31 embryos failing at least one criterion for every embryo satisfying all five. This is a mathematical boundary, not a model of PGT-P, which ordinarily ranks embryos using continuous polygenic scores.

05Moving frontiers

A useful future comparison has to let both selection and correction improve.

Common complex disease changes the character of the problem. There is no single affected genotype whose absence guarantees health; selection and editing both shift probabilities. The scale of any future benefit therefore depends on how far those probabilities can actually be moved.

Three capabilities move that frontier: causal knowledge, which determines what variants actually matter; embryo supply, which determines how many genomes can be selected among; and correction capacity, which determines how many relevant variants can be altered reliably and safely. Progress on one axis can strengthen either route without solving the others.

The permissive population-scaling scenario asks what happens if a larger future editing advantage is allowed. It applies a central 2.0% editing-advantage fraction to the modeled multifactorial birth-cohort component (with 0.8–3.5% parameter bounds). On that assumption set, the combined editing-relevant domain reaches about — birth-equivalent cases per annual cohort, or — of the default serious birth-cohort denominator.

That figure is a location on a moving map, not a forecast. Its model interval propagates the scenario’s parameter ranges; the larger uncertainty is whether the causal knowledge, multiplex correction and clinical performance assumed by the scenario will exist at all.

06Ethics & policy

The calculation does not choose the values. It shows where the values enter.

Once population impact, individual need, reproductive burden, technical risk, access and future option value are separated, the disagreement becomes easier to locate. Two people can accept the same empirical picture and still weight its parts differently.

The no-selectable-unaffected-embryo population makes the tension especially clear. Eleven thousand birth-equivalent cases are a small share of global serious genetic disease, yet correction could have unusual marginal value for a family whose embryos are all expected to carry a severe disorder. That need does not make an edit safer, but it explains why population priority and individual clinical justification can point in different directions.

Governance then becomes a problem of stages. A medically weak use, a scientifically premature indication, a preclinical research programme and a future clinical investigation are different regulatory objects. The strength of the medical indication can justify progressively more serious study while an independent safety threshold continues to govern movement toward clinical use.

Selection-First

When embryo selection can achieve the same important reproductive outcome with substantially lower heritable risk and an acceptable reproductive burden, selection has a defeasible presumption.

Somatic-First

When comparable benefit can be delivered to the future child without altering the heritable genome, the somatic route has a defeasible presumption.

1
Scale present impact

Expand access where established screening, diagnosis, reproductive medicine and treatment already change outcomes.

2
Develop the strongest frontier

Study severe indications where editing adds the most incremental medical value, while keeping the safety gate independent.

3
Recalculate as capability moves

Reassess selection, correction, risk reduction, resistance and enhancement as causal knowledge and technologies change.

Do not borrow the moral urgency of one use for another

Preventing a lethal monogenic disorder when no unaffected embryo can be selected begins from a different baseline from editing CCR5 for HIV resistance, reducing common-disease risk or altering a non-disease trait. Similar molecular tools do not make their medical alternatives or ethical justification equivalent.

07Methods & evidence

Every headline number should expose its unit, assumptions and epistemic status.

The research companion does not recompute epidemiology in the browser. It reads committed outputs from the same repository that generates the manuscript-facing results. The default run uses — Monte Carlo draws; downstream ratios are calculated within each draw before summarization.

Cited data, derived quantities, modeling assumptions, normative choices and provisional inputs are kept distinct. That is why Gate 2 is not promoted as a population percentage without patient-frequency weighting, why general embryo-editing efficiency and safety values remain unfilled, and why dollar cost-effectiveness claims are not promoted from exploratory inputs.
